Shenzhen Technology University, School of Design and Innovation | Environmental Design Thesis | Instructors: Cheng Zeng, Meikang Li | Spring 2025

This studio investigated how sensing, interaction, and environmental feedback could inform new forms of responsive spatial design. Students developed site-based design theses integrating Arduino-driven systems, environmental sensing, and real-time adaptive mechanisms to enhance human–environment relationships in urban public spaces.

**StateShift: Adaptive Urban Park — Huang Jiamin** Focusing on the urban residual spaces beneath Shenzhen’s overpasses, this design introduces rotating wall panels responsive to light intensity and crowd behavior. Using Arduino and light sensors, the project envisions a transformable social infrastructure where spatial form evolves with time, use, and public activity.

**Soundscape Healing Park — Chen Silan** Set in Liuxian Park, Shenzhen, this project explores how interactive acoustic devices can transform noise-polluted urban green spaces into therapeutic environments. Through sound sensors, micro-servos, and dynamic acoustic installations, the park becomes an adaptive “sound metabolism” system that mediates between noise and calm.

**Interactive Community Park — Mo Xiaotong** Anchored in Longgang District, this thesis proposes an interactive community park activated by temperature, humidity, and motion sensors. Responsive mist devices and water-collection systems create microclimates and playful feedback loops, enhancing comfort and engagement in high-density neighborhoods.

Across the studio, responsiveness was treated not as a technical gimmick but as a medium for empathy and environmental attunement—prompting students to rethink how design can sense, adapt, and heal.
